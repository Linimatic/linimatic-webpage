# Case study intake

A one-shot alternative to being interviewed: fill in what you have and hand it back. You don't need polished prose — notes, fragments, and raw numbers are better than smooth sentences. Leave anything you don't know blank; a blank becomes a follow-up question or a `[NEEDS: …]` marker in the draft, **never an invented fact**.

## Required — the floor

Without these three, the result is a brochure, not a case. If you can't supply one, say so explicitly rather than guessing.

**1. Customer + segment**
- Name (or an anonymized descriptor if you can't name them, e.g. "a leading European skylight maker"):
- Industry / what they do:
- Rough size (people, revenue, sites, volume — whatever conveys scale):

**2. Problem + stakes**
- What was hard, or what they needed:
- What it was costing them — or what would have happened if it stayed unsolved:

**3. At least one number**
- The hero outcome — the result the case lands on:
- *Or*, if no one had a baseline because no one else could do it: the difficulty in numbers (the tolerance, spec, scale, or constraint that made it hard):

**Pick the numbers that bite in your field:**
- Industrial: tolerances, downtime cost, scrap/yield, field returns, lead time
- SaaS: churn, activation time, latency, seats, support volume
- Services: hours saved, error rate, rework, time-to-launch
